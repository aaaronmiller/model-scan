1. Methodology Critique: ARDF v3.0 Single-Point Anchor
What ARDF v3.0 Does
Fixes one model (Fable 5, Adaptive Reasoning, Max Effort) at 100.
Synchronizes all other scores to this anchor via shortest-path median ratio through a bridge network of shared models across 9 benchmark sources.
Computes cost as normalized token use × model price, synchronized in log space to the same AA hub.
Derives the Pareto frontier: retain point i only when y_i > max(y_j) for every retained j with x_j ≤ x_i.
What Breaks
Problem
Symptom
Single-anchor fragility
If Fable 5's raw score shifts 2% across benchmark runs, every model in the dataset shifts 2%. The entire Y-axis is hostage to one model's noise.
Ceiling compression
Models scoring 95–100 on raw benchmarks all cluster in a 5-point band on the calibrated scale. The S+/S/A boundary becomes a knife-edge.
Free-model singularity
Cost = tokens × price. When price = $0 (free API tier), X = 0. On a log-scale X axis, this is undefined. On a linear scale, all free models collapse to a single vertical line. The Pareto frontier cannot distinguish between a free model that's genuinely cheap and one that's free but rate-limited to 50 requests/day.
Local-model blindness
A self-hosted 4B model on a laptop has $0/token API cost but required $1,500 hardware. ARDF v3.0 sees X = 0 and treats it as identical to a free API model with unlimited throughput. These are economically distinct.
No tier structure
The Pareto frontier is a continuous curve. It doesn't produce discrete tiers. IndyDevDan's S+/S/A/B/C/D structure is more actionable for model selection, but ARDF v3.0 has no mechanism to derive it.

The "free tier superstar" phenomenon is real but narrow. DeepSeek V4 Flash and MiMo 2.5 are free-tier superstars because they're open-weight AND have free API tiers. Their effective cost is the self-hosted cost ($0.0017), not the free API shadow cost ($0.005). The free API tier matters for users who can't self-host.

13. Important Limitations
Calibration makes score coordinates comparable under an explicit empirical mapping. It does not make the underlying benchmark tasks identical. The dashboard preserves source, metric, version, bridge path, anchor count, residual error, and confidence in every tooltip and export.
Estimated scores (⚠) are not benchmarks. Mythos 5 at 100.0 is an architectural equivalence assumption. Kimi K3 at 88.0 is a single-source extrapolation. Qwen 3.8 Max at 86.0 is a vendor-claim-adjacent estimate. These will be replaced with measured values as they enter the ARDF source network.
The three-regime cost model assumes representative hardware configurations. Actual self-hosted costs vary by GPU model, quantization level, batch size, and electricity rate. The reference configurations (Section 3.2) are medians, not guarantees.
IndyDevDan's tier placements are subjective and authoritative. Where ARDF scores disagree with tier placement (e.g., Gemini 3.1 Pro: ARDF 100.0, tier B), the tier placement wins. The ARDF score is evidence; the tier is judgment. Both are preserved.
The Pareto frontier is a snapshot. Model prices change weekly. New models release monthly. The frontier must be recomputed on each calibration update. The living document structure (Section 12) ensures this.

Lens
Challenge
Resolution
1
Opus 5 dominance
Opus 5 at $5/$25 dominates Fable 5 at $10/$50 on the Pareto frontier. Is Fable 5 now obsolete?
No. Fable 5 retains SWE-bench Pro (80.0% vs 79.2%) and HLE (53.3% vs 52.6%) edges. Mythos 5 retains HLE dominance (64.5%). Fable 5 is niche-optimal, not frontier-optimal.
2
Mythos 5 visibility
Still absent from ARDF source network. S+ placement rests on Anthropic positioning + HLE 64.5%.
Flagged ⚠. Will be resolved when AA publishes Mythos 5 as a distinct entry.
3
Gemini 3.6 Flash = 3.5 Flash on AA Index
Both score 50. Is the A-tier upgrade justified?
Yes. SWE-Pro 58.7%, TB 2.1 78.0%, OSWorld 83.0%, MLE-Bench 63.9%, 17% token reduction. The AA Index doesn't capture coding/agentic gains or efficiency.
4
Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite in C
AA Index 36 is low. Is C-tier too generous?
C-tier floor is 49 (Gemma 4 31B). Flash-Lite at 51 is barely above. But its $0.30/$2.50 API access and 54% TB 2.1 make it the cheapest API model above score 50. C-tier is correct.
5
Qwen 3.8 Max staleness
Still no benchmarks 6 days after release. S-tier at ⚠ 86 is a guess.
Acknowledged. Will be recalibrated when AA/BenchLM publish data. Could be anywhere from 80 to 98.
6
Opus 4.8 de-frontiering
Opus 4.8 was on the v3.1 frontier. Opus 5 removes it. Is this too aggressive?
No. Same price ($5/$25), strictly higher capability. This is textbook Pareto dominance. Opus 4.8 remains S-tier for users with existing integrations, but it's no longer the optimal choice.
7
Free-tier singularity (v3.1 carryover)
Does the three-regime cost model handle the new Flash-Lite correctly?
Yes. Flash-Lite's HF shadow cost ($0.005/task) places it above self-hosted models at the same score level. It's the cheapest zero-setup option, not the cheapest option overall.
8
PinchBench/Claw-Eval staleness
PinchBench snapshot is 07/10. Claw-Eval has 13 models. Neither includes Opus 5 or Gemini 3.6 Flash.
Acknowledged. These sources will be updated in v3.3 when new snapshots are published. Current data is directionally useful but not current.
9
SimpleBench gap
SimpleBench doesn't include Opus 5 yet. Fable 5 leads at 81.9%.
Will be updated when SimpleBench adds Opus 5. Expected to score ~82-83 based on AA Index parity.
10
Calibration continuity
Re-anchoring from Fable 5 = 100 to Opus 5/Fable 5 = 100 (co-equal) — does this break v3.1 comparability?
No. Fable 5 remains at 100. Opus 5 is added as a co-anchor at 100. All other scores are unchanged. The only shift is that Opus 5 now shares the ceiling rather than Fable 5 holding it alone.

One Synthesis
The July 21–25 window produced the most significant Pareto shift since Fable 5's June 9 release. Opus 5 at $5/$25 delivers Fable 5-class capability at Opus 4.8 pricing, collapsing the cost of frontier access by 50%. Gemini 3.6 Flash delivers the same intelligence as 3.5 Flash with 17% better token efficiency and materially stronger coding/agentic scores. The tier structure holds; the frontier shifted.
