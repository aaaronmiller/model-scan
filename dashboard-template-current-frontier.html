Does ARDF agree with Priors?

ARDF calculates the ten weighted components itself from the public AA payload. It also independently sums the nine evaluation task costs. Priors is queried with the supplied index, all efforts, strict coverage and separate API/subscription bases. Neither its scores nor its prices feed ARDF's primary line.

AA's published index freezes its Briefcase contribution; the displayed component can change. Consequently the published-index view and a fresh recomputation can differ even with the same formula. Additional differences arise from model eligibility, unresolved names or effort labels, rounding, missing costs and subscription assumptions. Rows with uncertain identities remain unresolved.

This is an independent implementation and source retrieval, not an independent benchmark experiment. The AA subtests are correlated. ARDF does not average the AA composite together with its own components or treat mirrors as additional independent evidence.

Short routing rules

  1. Use the right frontier. API task cost, subscription estimates and the value of expiring quota answer different questions. Filter for accessible routes before dispatch.
  2. Terra low / medium / high are useful. Terra xhigh is dominated by Sol medium on the general API workload; Terra max by Sol high. Start with Sol medium for a replacement of Terra xhigh unless the specific task or quota evidence favors Terra.
  3. Sonnet high remains useful within Claude. Its task-specific automation and terminal results matter. Sonnet xhigh/max generally loses to lower-effort Opus on the composite; this is not a universal ban on Sonnet or max effort.
  4. Use Opus medium/high for ambiguous integration and judgment. Escalate to xhigh for hard debugging. Use tests and completion evidence, not a benchmark score alone, to assess long-running agents.
  5. Fable is an escalation candidate, not the default worker. Use for unresolved reasoning or a genuinely different review when local results justify it. Its half-week cap is nested inside the shared quota, not additive.
  6. Scouting starts with deterministic tools. Give cheap/free workers bounded file packs and require path-linked evidence. Avoid expensive repeated context handoffs.
  7. Review against evidence. Require a concrete defect, trigger, location and test. Different model families offer another perspective, not guaranteed correctness.
  8. During the final quota window, optimize accepted work before reset. Favor valuable bounded completion and independent verification over minimizing API-equivalent dollars or consuming quota for its own sake.
